# M4 — macOS video surface + Metal presenter: tester checklist

What this proves: the remote screen shows inside the app window on a Mac, sharp on
Retina, with the hover toolbar, input, file drop and the Settings dialog all
behaving like the Windows build. Everything here needs a real Mac with a display;
the shader itself is already covered by automated tests.

## Before you start

* **Mac:** macOS 13 Ventura or later. Note the model (Apple Silicon / Intel), the
  macOS version, and whether the screen is Retina. If you can, also try one
  non-Retina external monitor (§6).
* **Build:** a CI-built DMG from `port/multi-os` that includes M4 **and** a
  decoder: M3 (VideoToolbox) or D2 (software H.264). Without a decoder the viewer
  stops before the video view appears. The release notes / CI run name the tasks
  it contains.
* **Install:** open the DMG and drag ShareCtrlScreen to Applications. On first
  launch macOS says it cannot verify the developer: System Settings → Privacy &
  Security → scroll down → **Open Anyway** → Open.
* **Host:** a Windows PC running the current Windows build, sharing its screen.
  Put a window with small text (Notepad with a 10 pt font) and a page with
  clear left/right/top/bottom edges (a full-screen Paint canvas works) on it.
* **Log:** `~/Library/Logs/ShareCtrlScreen/app.log`. Keep it open in Terminal:
  `tail -f ~/Library/Logs/ShareCtrlScreen/app.log`.

For every step write **pass / fail**, and for a fail a screenshot (Cmd+Shift+4)
plus the log lines around that time.

## 1. The picture appears

1. Connect from the Mac to the Windows host with control enabled.
2. Expected: the remote desktop fills the window under the title bar, upright
   (the Windows taskbar at the bottom), colours like on the PC itself (not
   washed out or too dark), with black bars only where the aspect ratios
   differ. No white flash, no web page showing through.
3. Expected in the log, in this order: `video view: created (…x… px)`,
   `presenter: Metal on <GPU name>, Bgra8Unorm, Immediate`,
   `presenter: first frame presented (W x H)`. Note W×H.
4. **Retina sizes:** W×H must equal the window's content size in *pixels*
   (points × 2 on Retina). Check: the log's `viewer: requesting WxH from host`
   line about a second after connecting should show the same numbers. On a
   1440×900-point window on Retina that is about 2880×(1800 minus title bar).
5. Text on the host (Notepad) must be crisp — not blurry, not doubled. Compare
   with a screenshot taken on the Windows PC.

## 2. Hover toolbar (top edge)

1. Move the pointer slowly up to the very top edge of the video (just below
   the title bar). Expected: the session toolbar (Disconnect, settings…)
   appears over the top 40 points of the video **the moment** the pointer is
   within the top 2 pixels — the picture does **not** move, shrink or slide;
   only its top strip is covered. (If instead the picture jumps up by the bar
   height, or its *bottom* strip disappears, report it: that is the layer
   gravity being upside down on this macOS version.)
2. Click a toolbar button (e.g. the settings icon). Expected: it reacts — the
   click goes to the toolbar, not to the remote PC.
3. Move the pointer back down into the video. Expected: the toolbar stays
   while the pointer is less than ~56 points from the top (about 16 points
   below the bar), then disappears; the picture again fills the top.
4. Flick the pointer quickly up and out through the top of the window.
   Expected: the toolbar appears (a fast flick counts as reaching the edge).
5. With the toolbar shown, check the log for a new `viewer: requesting …`
   line. Expected: **none** — showing the toolbar must not change the size the
   host encodes at.
6. Repeat 1–3 on a host whose screen is static (nothing moving). Expected:
   the same, and the picture still does not jump (no new frame is needed).

## 3. Settings dialog over the video

1. During the session, open Settings from the toolbar.
2. Expected: the Settings dialog is fully visible and usable — not hidden
   behind the video, not cut into a 40-point strip. You can type in its text
   fields straight away without clicking them first (if you must click first,
   note it).
3. Close Settings. Expected: the video comes back, toolbar hidden.
4. Repeat with the toolbar revealed at the moment you open Settings.

## 4. Mouse

1. Move over the remote. Expected: the remote cursor follows exactly under
   yours; at the four corners of the picture it reaches the remote's corners.
2. With black bars visible (make the window very wide or very tall), click in
   the bar next to an edge. Expected: the click lands on the nearest edge of
   the remote screen, not offset.
3. Left click, right click (two-finger click on a trackpad), and middle click
   (if you have a 3-button mouse). Expected: the right thing on the host
   (context menu for right click).
4. Click-and-drag a window on the host by its title bar, including dragging
   past the edge of our window. Expected: it follows and drops where released.
5. Click our window while another app is in front. Expected: that first
   click also reaches the remote (and our window comes to the front).

## 5. Scroll

1. Mouse wheel: one notch should scroll the host about 3 lines (like a wheel
   on the PC itself). Note the direction.
2. Trackpad two-finger scroll, slow and fast. Expected: smooth, slow
   movements still scroll (not ignored), direction matches how pages scroll
   locally on the Mac (with "natural scrolling" on and off — System Settings →
   Mouse / Trackpad).
3. Horizontal scroll (shift+wheel or trackpad sideways) in a wide page on the
   host. Expected: moves the same way as locally. **Note the direction** —
   this one is the most likely to be inverted.

## 6. Retina ↔ non-Retina

1. With an external non-Retina monitor: drag the window from the Retina
   screen to the external one mid-session.
2. Expected: picture stays sharp on both; the log shows a new
   `viewer: requesting …` with about half the pixel size; the toolbar still
   reveals at the top edge and is 40 points tall on both screens.

## 7. Keyboard

1. Click into the video, open Notepad on the host, type `Hello World 123`
   with Shift for the capitals. Expected: exactly that text.
2. Hold a letter: Expected: it auto-repeats on the host.
3. With **"Capture system shortcuts" off** (the default), press Cmd+Q **while
   the video has focus**. Expected: the app **quits** (M12: Cmd+Q is the local
   escape hatch and never goes to the remote; before M12 it did). The host
   gets no Q. Re-launch and reconnect. Also with capture off, Cmd+C on the
   video still reaches the host (Ctrl+C with translation on).
4. Hold Option (Alt), press Cmd+Tab to another Mac app, release everything,
   come back. Then type on the host. Expected: no stuck Alt/Cmd/Shift on the
   host (letters arrive unmodified).
5. Caps Lock on, type, Caps Lock off. Expected: host toggles with you.
6. In a view-only session (control off): Cmd+Q and other Mac shortcuts work
   locally while the pointer is over the video.

## 8. File drop

1. Drag a file from Finder onto the video. Expected: green "+" copy badge
   while hovering; on drop the file transfers to the host and is pasted into
   the window under the drop point (e.g. drop on an open Explorer folder).
2. In a view-only session (no file transfer): Expected: the drag is refused
   (no badge, the file icon slides back).

## 9. Window life cycle

1. Resize the window by dragging a corner during a session. Expected: picture
   follows live with correct letterbox; after you stop, one
   `viewer: requesting …` line (debounced).
2. Full screen (green button) and back. Expected: same.
3. Minimise to the Dock and restore. Expected: picture resumes, no freeze;
   no flood of `render error` lines in the log.
4. Disconnect. Expected: video disappears, home page usable; reconnect works
   and the picture is correct again.

## Send back

The pass/fail list, the Mac model + macOS version, screenshots for any fail,
and the whole `app.log`.
